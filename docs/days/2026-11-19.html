Stocks

S&P 500 slips as tech's early gains fade ahead of Anthropic's debut

The S&P 500 (a index of 500 large US companies, used to track the market) had fallen five days in a row through Wednesday. Investors were digesting NVIDIA's earnings (the company's profit report) and waiting for Anthropic's IPO (the first time a company sells its shares to the public). Futures (contracts that show where the market may open) pointed higher this morning. The Nasdaq 100 fund was up 2.06% at 10:04 a.m. ET. By 11:05 a.m. ET, it was up only 0.41%. The Dow did worse than the S&P. Humana and UnitedHealth fell on a proposed CMS audit programme. CMS is the US agency that runs Medicare. The audit targets Medicare Advantage overpayments (extra payments insurers may have received). Investors also worried about retroactive clawbacks (having to pay back money for past years). UnitedHealth is a Dow member, so its drop pulled the index down. Anthropic is the winner of the day in terms of money raised. CNBC said the IPO had failed to revive excitement for AI stocks. Health insurers with large Medicare Advantage businesses are the losers until the rules are clearer. Watch Anthropic's first-day trading and the final form of the CMS proposal.

Rates

Fed rate hike bets firm up while US factory data weakens

The Fed (the US central bank) held its rate at 3.75%-4.00% last month. Markets now expect a hike. CME FedWatch (a tool that shows what traders expect the Fed to do) showed about a 90% chance of a quarter-point rise and 10% for a half-point rise at the December 8-9 meeting. St. Louis Fed's Musalem said several increases may be needed before spring 2027. Today's data mixed growth and price signals. The Philadelphia Fed index fell to 6.3, its lowest since May, from 20.9. This index measures how factories in the region are doing. Prices received (what factories are charging for their goods) reached their highest level since June 2022. Weaker growth with rising prices makes the Fed's job harder. The 10-year yield (the interest rate on 10-year government bonds) eased slightly but stays high. Winners are savers and the dollar. Losers are rate-sensitive sectors (industries that do badly when rates rise) and non-yielding assets (things that pay no interest or income, such as gold). Watch core PCE (the Fed's preferred inflation gauge) next Wednesday and any further Fed speeches.

Commodities

Gold slides below $3,700 on yields and a firm dollar

Gold hit a record $5,589.38 in January. On Wednesday it fell below $3,800 for the first time since September 2025. Today it dropped below $3,700. The old idea of gold as a hedge against inflation (protection when prices rise) and geopolitics (protection against world conflict) has weakened. The conflict pushed oil and inflation higher. This fuelled bets on higher interest rates. Those rate bets lift the dollar and make non-yielding gold less attractive. Miners and gold ETF holders (people who own funds that track gold prices) lose the most. TSX materials stocks fell 5.4% on Wednesday. Analysts disagree on what comes next. One expects $3,500 to $3,600. The World Gold Council points to $3,605 as a possible support level (a price where buyers may step in). Watch the dollar, Treasury yields and core PCE.

Acquisition · About ¥1.84T (~$11.6B)

Mitsui Chemicals → JSR

Mitsui is paying roughly 16 times expected EBITDA (a measure of company earnings before some costs). The deal is financed by a ¥1.64T bridge loan (a short-term loan used until longer-term funding is arranged). This temporarily pushes net debt (total debt minus cash) above four times EBITDA. That implies Mitsui will need to refinance the bridge loan with longer-term debt. The logic is to move into semiconductor materials, which are tied to chip demand. The high multiple shows buyers will pay up for AI-linked supply chains. Closing is expected in the second half of the fiscal year ending March 2028, subject to regulatory approvals (permission from governments).

Words to know

Rate hike

When the central bank raises its key interest rate to make borrowing more expensive and slow the economy.

Yield

The interest rate a bond pays, shown as a percentage of its price.

IPO

Initial public offering, the first time a private company sells shares to the public on a stock exchange.

Safe haven

An asset that investors buy to protect their money when markets are scary.

Opportunity cost

What you give up by choosing one option over another, such as the interest you could earn instead of holding gold.

Bridge loan

A short-term loan used to pay for something until longer-term funding is arranged.
