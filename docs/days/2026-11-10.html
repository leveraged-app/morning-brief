Markets

Stocks surge as the AI trade rebounds after last week's rout

Last week investors dumped AI and chip stocks, and the Nasdaq fell into correction territory before a partial recovery. The trigger was worry that Democratic midterm wins could slow the construction of AI data centres.

The rebound was broad. AMD rose 9.22% and Marvell gained 9.07%. The Dow rose 1,104 points. About $1.5 trillion of market value was added. The VIX fell 22.31% to 19.92.

Lower oil helped. Trump said a resumption of strikes on Iran was highly unlikely if negotiations progress. Nvidia was the exception. It fell about 5% after its board launched a probe. Chipmakers, megacaps and industrials gained. Nvidia holders and energy producers lagged.

The S&P 500 is still 3.4% below its record high. Watch whether the rally holds through today's inflation data.

Yields edge up as CPI looms and Fed hike bets build

The Fed held rates at 3.75%–4.00% in October, with two policymakers preferring a hike. Employers then added 165,000 jobs, well above the 65,000 forecast, and unemployment fell to 4.1%.

Hike odds depend on the source and timing. One reading put the odds at 76.8% after the jobs report. Another had them at 85% on Monday afternoon. The US 10-year yield rose 2 bps to 4.86%. Canada's 10-year fell 2 bps to 3.65%.

Economists expect headline CPI up about 0.3% on the month and core up 0.2%, after 0.6% and 0.3% in September. There is no official annual rate, because the 2025 shutdown meant no October 2025 CPI was published.

A 0.3% core reading would likely lift Treasury yields and the dollar and add pressure to tech stocks. US bond markets are closed Wednesday for Veterans Day. Producer prices follow Friday.

Markets

TSX rebounds on metals as Barrick picks New York for its IPO

Monday's 1.8% gain was the TSX's highest close since early September. Industrials and financials also rose. Energy fell 1.0% as crude dropped.

Rates also mattered. The Bank of Canada's survey of market participants sees the policy rate going from 2.25% to 2.50% in December and 2.75% by March 2027. A strong 10-year bond auction supported the idea of gradual tightening.

With Q3 results, Barrick said it expects North American Barrick to list on the NYSE. The IPO is planned for later this year or early 2027. The plan is a primary listing in New York and a secondary one in Toronto. The Globe and Mail calls it a setback for Canadian capital markets.

Watch the IPO timing and whether index providers let the spinoff into Canadian indexes.

Acquisition · C$2.7

Strathcona Resources → Greenfire Resources.

Greenfire holders can take 0.25 Strathcona shares (about C$9.09) or C$11.00 in cash per share. The cash option is a 39% premium to the November 6 close. Waterous Energy Fund holds 72% of Greenfire and 57% of Strathcona. It backs the deal and plans to take shares.

The combined company would have over 3.0 billion barrels of proved plus probable reserves and 2026 production of 153,000 barrels per day. That fits the push for scale and reserve life among Canadian oil producers.

The deal needs two-thirds shareholder approval plus minority approval. Meetings are expected in January 2027. A single backer sitting on both sides makes the minority vote worth watching.

Full story

Acquisition · $11.6 billion

Mitsui Chemicals → JSR.

Mitsui plans to fund the deal with borrowing, equity issuance and other capital raising. DBJ's stake would later convert into non-voting preferred shares.

Mitsui expects the combination to add about 40 billion yen of annual profit. The size of the financing has raised balance-sheet questions, because the deal is worth far more than Mitsui's market value. It shows governments backing domestic champions in semiconductor supply chains.

Full story

Acquisition · $4 billion

LS Power → NRG gas plants.

The plants are in Texas, New York, Rhode Island and Connecticut, with almost 5 GW of capacity. With a pending Constellation purchase, LS Power's gas fleet would grow from 2.2 GW to 11.5 GW.

Scotiabank and Evercore advised LS Power. The deal is expected to close next year, subject to regulatory approvals. It signals strong buyer appetite for firm power as AI data-centre demand grows.
