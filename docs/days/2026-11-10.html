Hot inflation puts a December Fed rate hike on the table

Why it happened

October CPI (the Consumer Price Index, a common measure of inflation) rose 0.4%. Economists had expected 0.3%. Short-term Treasury yields (the interest rates on short-term US government loans) jumped right after the report.

Why it matters

A rate hike makes borrowing more expensive for companies and for deals. Fast-growing tech stocks, whose value depends on profits far in the future, usually take the biggest hit.

Commodities2 of 3

Gold drops as bond yields and the dollar rise

Why it happened

Stronger inflation data pushed Treasury yields and the dollar higher. Gold gave up recent gains. Some investors also sold to take profits after Monday's rally.

Why it matters

When yields and the dollar rise, holding gold becomes less attractive. Gold pays no interest, so the interest you could earn elsewhere becomes a bigger cost (this is called opportunity cost).

Why it matters

Crypto often moves like a bet on cheap money. When rate hikes look likely, it can fall harder than stocks.

Macro

Hot inflation puts a December Fed rate hike on the table

Monday was the S&P 500's best day in seven months. That came just two trading days after its worst day in 18 months. Part of the swing came from investors moving money out of AI stocks and into defensive sectors (steady industries like healthcare that people need in good times and bad). Today's report took back some of that relief. Core prices (inflation without food and energy, which can jump around a lot) rose 0.3% in October. That was hotter than expected. Core prices have risen 0.3% or more in 5 of the first 10 months of 2026. Futures (bets on where prices will go) now fully price in a 25 basis point hike (a quarter of a percentage point) by the Fed in December. Fed Governor Waller said even a 50 basis point move could be right. The split was clear when the market opened. The Nasdaq (a tech-heavy stock index) fell, while the Russell 2000 (a index of smaller US companies) rose. That fits money leaving crowded AI trades. Leveraged funds (investment funds that borrow money to bet bigger) and momentum traders (people who buy stocks that are already rising) were already losing money. Nvidia fell 4.3% on Monday after reports about CEO Jensen Huang's alleged links to a woman suspected of Chinese intelligence ties. Watch what Fed speakers say, and Friday's PPI (producer prices, a measure of inflation at the wholesale level). The next Fed meeting is on December 9.

Commodities

Gold drops as bond yields and the dollar rise

Gold had just hit a three-week high. A weaker dollar had helped support it. That help faded once the CPI report made the dollar stronger, since higher US interest rates make the dollar more attractive. Gold was already getting weaker before the data came out. Hawkish comments from the Fed (comments that suggest higher rates) pushed it down ahead of CPI. Silver fell more than 3% to $51.72 after both headline and core CPI came in above forecasts. Gold miners (companies that dig gold out of the ground) and people who own gold through ETFs (exchange-traded funds, which are funds you can buy like a stock) lose out when real yields (interest rates after inflation) rise. Businesses that buy goods priced in other currencies also pay more when the dollar gets stronger. What happens next depends on how strongly the Fed sounds. A 50 basis point hike in December would add more pressure. Softer data would let gold recover.

Crypto

Bitcoin hits a near three-month low

Bitcoin fell below $75,000. It had just posted its lowest weekly close since August. Investors pulled $1.28 billion out of spot bitcoin ETFs in one week. Also, $1.04 billion of leveraged longs (bets that prices will rise, made with borrowed money) were forced to close. Reports say about $200 million more in leveraged bets closed within five minutes of the CPI release. Smaller coins fell further. Ether, XRP and Solana were each down closer to 6%. Companies that hold bitcoin are under pressure too. Strategy, the largest listed company holding bitcoin, is now trading below the average price it paid for its bitcoin. Watch the ETF money flows (whether money is going in or out) and whether another wave of forced selling of leveraged bets happens. Stock moves after the Fed speakers will matter too, since crypto has been moving with how willing investors are to take risks.

Words to know

Inflation (CPI)

How fast the prices of everyday things like food, rent and fuel are rising.

Federal Reserve (the Fed)

The US central bank, which sets the main interest rate that affects borrowing across the economy.

Yields

The interest return on a bond, which moves up when prices fall and rates rise.

Risk-off

A mood where investors sell riskier assets like stocks and crypto and move money into safer ones.

Basis point (bp)

One hundredth of a percentage point, so 25 bp is a quarter of a percentage point.

IPO (Initial Public Offering)

The first time a private company sells shares to the public on a stock exchange.
