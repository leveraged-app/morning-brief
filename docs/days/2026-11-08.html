Stocks

AI and chip stocks get hammered as the VIX jumps

Wednesday's post-midterm rally lifted the Dow 1,569 points. By Thursday's close the Dow was 1,833 points lower, which erased all of those gains.

The S&P 500 fell 334.89 points, or 4.36%, to 7,353.73. That was its worst second day after a midterm in nearly a century. Tech fell 7.3%, and the Philadelphia chip index lost 12%. The VIX rose 69.66% to 28.18.

The AI-heavy names were hit hardest. Nvidia makes up about 8% of the SPY fund, while the Dow has less AI exposure. Dip-buyers returned on Friday. The Nasdaq gained 1.09% to 24,579.42, and Micron rose 5% after a 17% drop the day before.

What to watch: whether volatility settles or builds. The S&P 500 was higher a month later after nine of the past 10 VIX jumps above 50%. That is history, not a forecast. Cisco and Applied Materials report Thursday after the close, which gives fresh read-throughs on AI demand.

Rates

Treasuries rally on haven demand, but Fed hike bets linger

The Fed raised rates in September for the first time since 2023, after rising oil prices pushed inflation higher. It then held at its latest meeting. Oil has been the swing factor. It fell more than 7% on Monday on hopes of renewed talks to end the Middle East war.

The 10-year yield ended down 6 bps at 4.84%. Over the week, the 2-year yield fell 13 bps to 4.48%, and the 30-year was flat at 5.31%. That steepened the curve as traders trimmed bets on aggressive hikes.

Friday's jobs data pulled the other way. The US added 165,000 jobs in October, against a forecast of about 59,000. Upward revisions put the three-month average at 137,000. Yields climbed and rate-hike odds rose.

Bondholders and rate-sensitive borrowers gain from lower yields. Anyone leaning on near-term Fed cuts loses. The Treasury sells $58B of 3-year notes Monday at 1:00 p.m. ET, and CPI lands Tuesday.

Canada

Loonie slides as Canada sheds jobs again

The Bank of Canada raised its rate by 25 bps to 2.50% and warned that more increases might be needed if energy costs broaden into inflation. The jobs miss complicates that story.

USD/CAD rose 0.89% to 1.4113. The loonie hit its weakest level since October 19 and was on track to lose nearly 1% on the week, after three straight weekly gains. Canada's 2-year yield fell, and the gap to the US 2-year widened to about 139 bps.

Scotiabank strategists said elevated equity volatility overshadowed everything else, with the jobs data adding a nudge lower. Exporters and Canadians holding US assets benefit from a weaker loonie. Importers and US-bound travellers lose.

Watch Monday's 10:30 a.m. ET Bank of Canada Market Participants Survey, US CPI, and oil headlines.

Financing · $7.5B

Apple → investment-grade bond investors

Orders peaked at about $31 billion, more than four times the deal size. The 2036 notes priced about 55 bps over Treasuries, inside recent deals from Morgan Stanley, Meta and Oracle. Bank of America, Deutsche Bank, Goldman Sachs and JPMorgan led it. Proceeds go to general corporate purposes, including buybacks.

The logic is capital structure, not need. The CFO said Apple no longer targets net cash neutral. Big tech is increasingly tapping debt markets to fund AI capacity, despite historically relying on cash. Tight pricing shows credit investors still treat top-tier tech as safe.

Acquisition · US$2.9B

AMD → Taalas

AMD disclosed the price in a Wednesday filing. It is one of the largest takeovers of a Canadian tech company. The consideration is cash and stock, and AMD first announced the deal in August. It still needs regulatory approval and other conditions. Filings show a big markup in Taalas's valuation.

The deal signals that strategic buyers still pay up for AI hardware assets, even as public chip stocks sell off. For Canada, it is a large exit for a homegrown startup.

IPO · $214.3M

vVardis Holding → NYSE

The Swiss company launched Friday, offering 9.52 million shares at $21–$24, which raises $214.29M at the $22.50 midpoint. It is set to price next week, with Goldman Sachs, J.P. Morgan, William Blair, UBS and Deutsche Bank among the lead managers.

A private-equity-backed healthcare-adjacent listing is a useful read on appetite outside tech. Pricing versus range will show how much the VIX spike has hurt new issues. Anthropic is reportedly preparing an IPO roadshow after filing its S-1 on October 26.

Your interview line

An AI-led selloff took the S&P 500 down 4.36% and sent the VIX up almost 70%. Treasuries rallied, but strong jobs data kept a December Fed hike in play, so CPI is the next catalyst.
Heavy AI infrastructure spending now meets investor doubt about returns. A tech or utility client should stress-test its data-center capex under slower-demand scenarios and think about how to fund it, as Apple's bond deal shows.
AMD would apply the acquisition method to Taalas. That means measuring cash and stock consideration at fair value, recognizing goodwill, and later testing it for impairment. Auditors would focus on those fair-value inputs and on impairment triggers after the market drop. For Apple's bonds, they would check that issuance costs are netted against the debt and amortized using the effective interest method.
