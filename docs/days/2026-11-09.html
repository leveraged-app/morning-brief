Stocks

Stocks surge as tech rebounds and crude slides

Last week's rout followed the U.S. midterm elections. Investors dumped AI and chip stocks and pushed the Nasdaq into correction territory before a partial recovery on Friday. Monday's move extends that bounce.

The S&P 500 is up 2.43% and the Nasdaq 2.74%. Energy was the only S&P sector in the red, and industrials led the rest. Brent has come down from above $112 at the start of the month amid uncertainty over the war with Iran. One report had AMD and Marvell each up roughly 9%.

Chipmakers, industrials and airlines-type oil consumers benefit from lower crude, while energy producers lose. A strategist quoted by CNBC expects the S&P 500 to stay rangebound through year-end, citing elevated oil and uncertainty over the Fed.

Watch Tuesday's CPI and CoreWeave's earnings, which will test whether the AI trade can keep its footing.

Stocks

Nvidia slides as board probes CEO Huang

A committee of independent directors, advised by outside counsel, will lead the review. The board says it has no reason to believe national security or company secrets were compromised. Nvidia says Huang intends to remain CEO during the investigation.

The shares fell as much as about six per cent and hit their lowest intraday level since March 31. A 4.3% drop would erase roughly $193 billion of market value. The stock had already fallen almost 20% over the past month in the broader AI selloff.

Most of the market shrugged it off, and many megacap tech names rose. Nvidia's suppliers and customers are the group to watch for contagion.

Next up is Nvidia's 17 November earnings report, which will show how management handles the issue and how investors react.

Rates

US and Canadian yields split on jobs data

The U.S. 10-year yield is at 4.86%, up 2 bps, and Canada's is 3.65%, down 2 bps. Traders see 78% odds of a quarter-point Fed hike in December. The Fed raised rates in September and paused in October.

The gap shows up in currencies too. The Canadian dollar weakened to near 1.41 per U.S. dollar after the diverging jobs reports. A firmer U.S. dollar also raises costs for Canadian firms with USD debt, while exporters gain.

Tuesday's CPI is the next test. Forecasts call for 0.3% headline and 0.2% core month-over-month. A hot print would raise hike odds and could pressure equity valuations on both sides of the border. A soft one would support the rebound.

Anthropic → Nasdaq listing

Strathcona: Greenfire holders can take 0.25 Strathcona share or C$11 cash per share. Strathcona closed at $36.34 and Greenfire at $7.93 on Friday. By my math, the cash option is roughly a 39% premium. Waterous Energy Fund owns 72% of Greenfire and 57% of Strathcona and backs the deal. Strathcona expects about $400 million of cash tax savings over four years. Shareholder meetings are expected in January 2027.

Mitsui/JSR: Mitsui plans to fund the purchase with borrowing, equity issuance and other measures. It expects about 40 billion yen of extra annual profit. It would be Japan's largest chemicals-sector acquisition. Questions have been raised about Mitsui's balance sheet.

Anthropic: That range is below earlier talk of nearly $2 trillion. Goldman Sachs and Morgan Stanley reportedly lead a syndicate of 22 banks. The company reportedly aims to finish before Thanksgiving. The pricing will set a public benchmark for AI valuations.

Your interview line

It's a relief rally led by tech and lower oil, but a possible December Fed hike and Tuesday's CPI keep the market fragile.
