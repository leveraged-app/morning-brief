Stocks

Stocks stay under pressure despite a strong Nvidia report

The S&P 500 was down 3.2% over the month through Tuesday. Investors are unsure about the AI trade (bets on companies that use artificial intelligence to grow). A scandal at Nvidia led to the departure of its CEO, Jensen Huang. Nvidia's report was the first test since he left. Revenue was $107.6B, above the $101.87B that analysts expected (the consensus). Its guidance (its forecast) for Q4 revenue is $130B, about 4% above what people expected. Other chip companies such as AMD jumped. Nvidia's own shares still gave back their gains. The Dow fell by more than 1%. The S&P 500 ended Tuesday at 7,340.53. That is just under its 200-day average of 7,346.64 (the average price over the last 200 trading days). BTIG's technician, an analyst who studies price charts, says the next stop could be the 7,000 to 7,100 zone. Bulls (investors who expect prices to rise) argue that big tech companies have not cut their spending on computing plans, and demand for computing power is still solid. Chip suppliers and AI infrastructure companies are the winners so far. Software companies and growth stocks that are sensitive to interest rates are the losers. Watch the Fed minutes (notes from the central bank's last meeting) at 2:00 PM and Palo Alto Networks' results tonight.

Commodities

Gold sinks to its lowest level in over a year

Gold fell below $3,800 on Wednesday. That is its lowest level in about 14 months. Bets on rate hikes lifted the dollar. The dollar is near a 19-month high, which makes gold more expensive for buyers who use other currencies. This slide is the opposite of a classic safe-haven move (when investors run to gold during trouble). Gold is roughly 33% below its January record of $5,595. Yahoo Finance, citing CME FedWatch (a tool that tracks market bets on Fed moves), put the chance of a rate increase in December at 96.7%. The next Fed decision is on December 9. Gold miners, including Canadian gold producers, lose when prices fall. Importers and people who hold dollars gain. The World Gold Council flags $3,605 as a possible support level (a price where buyers may step in). Watch the Fed minutes this afternoon for how strongly the Fed leans toward another rate hike.

Crypto

Bitcoin slides further as rate bets and ETF outflows bite

Bitcoin is around 26% below its October high. It crashed toward $62,000 over the weekend. It closed below its 200-week moving average (a long-term price average) near $68K. It also failed to follow stock futures higher after Nvidia's beat. The popular basis trade (a strategy that earns a small profit from the gap between bitcoin prices in two markets) now yields only about 3 to 5% a year. That is near or below Treasury yields (the interest paid on US government bonds). This removes a source of steady demand. The Fear & Greed Index (a score of investor mood from 0 to 100) sits at 11, which is extreme fear. Leveraged long holders (investors who borrowed money to bet on prices rising) and ETF buyers are the losers. Strategy's average purchase price was $75,451 on 849,327 BTC. That means its position is underwater, worth less than it paid. Wintermute warns that a drop below $60,000 is possible. Watch ETF flows and whether bitcoin can get back above the 200-week line.

Words to know

Rate hike

When the central bank raises its key interest rate to slow the economy and fight rising prices.

Yield

The interest rate an investment pays, shown as a percentage of its price.

Safe haven

An investment that people buy when they are scared, hoping it will hold its value.

ETF (exchange-traded fund)

A fund that holds a basket of assets, like bitcoin or stocks, and trades on an exchange like a single stock.

Liquidation

When a broker forces the sale of a borrowed bet because its value fell too far.

IPO (initial public offering)

The first time a private company sells its shares to the public on a stock exchange.
