1. The numbers

It was a risk-off tone, led by a sharp AI and chip selloff. The S&P 500 fell 4.36% to 7,353.73, its biggest one-day drop since August 2011 apart from the 2020 Covid crash and the April 2025 tariff shock. The VIX jumped to 28.18. Those figures are Thursday's close. Friday brought a partial rebound, and some other rows in the table (Nasdaq, oil, the dollar index) don't line up with the sources I found, so treat the table with care.

2. What moved and why

AI chip stocks crashed after the US midterms
Why it happened: Democrats won control of Congress, and candidates in both parties campaigned against AI data centers. That raised doubts about the data center build-out. The semiconductor index dropped 12%, its worst day since March 2020, and Nvidia fell 9%.
Why it matters: The damage was concentrated. The typical S&P 500 stock was barely off its high. A few giant, AI-linked companies can drive the whole index.

A strong US jobs report revived Fed rate-hike bets
Why it happened: The US added 165,000 jobs in October against a 65,000 consensus, wages rose 0.4% and unemployment dipped to 4.1%. Futures put the odds of a December Fed hike near 75%. Yields and the dollar rose, and gold slipped.
Why it matters: Rates are still the main driver across asset classes. Good economic news can be bad news for bonds and gold when the Fed is leaning toward hikes.

Weak Canadian jobs data pushed the loonie lower
Why it happened: Canada lost 4,700 jobs in October, when a gain of 13,600 was expected, and USD/CAD rose to about 1.4110. The unemployment rate rose to 6.7%.
Why it matters: A softer labour market gives the Bank of Canada less reason to keep hiking.

3. What's coming today

  • Saturday: markets are closed, and I found no major data releases, central bank events or big earnings scheduled.
  • Monday: Chicago Fed President Goolsbee speaks at 10:00 AM ET. Barrick Mining and Constellation Energy are expected to report in the morning.
  • Tuesday: US October CPI is due at 8:30 ET. This is the main event next week.

4. Canada corner

  • TSX level and change aren't available in my data, so I can't say how Canadian stocks closed.
  • The Bank of Canada recently raised rates. Governor Macklem said further hikes may be needed but would be decided meeting by meeting.
  • After the jobs miss, the market-implied chance of a December BoC hike fell to 35% from 40%. The Canada 10-year yield ended at 3.67%.
  • The loonie was on track to lose nearly 1% for the week, ending three straight weekly gains.
  • Enbridge was expected to report earnings Friday morning.

5. The takeaway

If someone asks you about markets today: "The selloff was mostly an AI-and-chips story tied to US political risk, not a broad collapse. Meanwhile, strong jobs data keeps rate-hike expectations alive, so next week's CPI matters."
