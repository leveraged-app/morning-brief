1. The numbers

This was a risk-off week that ended with a partial rebound. Tech sold off hard, and the Nasdaq bounced on Friday. The volatility gauge stayed elevated. *Data note: the S&P 500 and VIX figures in the table match Thursday's close. The Nasdaq figure is Friday's.* Thursday's close was 7,353.73 for the S&P 500, down 4.4%, with the VIX up 69.7% to 28.18. The VIX fell 9.01% to 25.64 on Friday.

2. What moved and why

AI stocks crashed on fears of tighter regulation
Why it happened: Investors feared that Democratic midterm wins would bring tighter US rules for the AI industry. The chip index fell 12.25%, its biggest one-day drop since March 2020.
Why it matters: The Nasdaq's Friday gain reclaimed only some of the damage. Valuations that rest on AI spending are now being questioned.

A strong US jobs report raised the odds of a Fed rate hike
Why it happened: Payrolls rose 165,000, against about 65,000 expected. Unemployment fell to 4.1%.
Why it matters: After the report, futures implied roughly an 80% chance of a Fed hike on December 8–9. Good news for the economy is now bad news for rate-sensitive assets. I couldn't verify the 6 bp fall in the US 10-year yield shown in the table.

Oil swung on US–Iran diplomacy hopes
Why it happened: Brent fell more than 16% on the week and US crude lost nearly 13%. Part of the reason was fresh hopes for a diplomatic breakthrough to end the Mideast conflict.
Why it matters: Oil feeds inflation, which feeds Fed and Bank of Canada decisions. *Data note: the table shows WTI at 99.64, but CNBC reports a settle of $90.83. Check this before quoting it.*

3. What's coming today

  • Today is Saturday, so markets are closed. I found no major scheduled data, central bank events or earnings.
  • Next week, Tuesday brings the October US CPI report.
  • CoreWeave, Applied Materials and Cisco report earnings. Applied Materials and Cisco report Thursday after the close.
  • Statistics Canada releases October inflation on November 16.

4. Canada corner

  • The TSX isn't in the table. It closed Thursday at 35,605.27, down 2.03%. It gained 0.60% on Friday.
  • Canada lost 4,700 jobs in October, and unemployment rose to 6.7%. That cooled expectations for Bank of Canada hikes.
  • The loonie weakened. It touched its weakest level since October 19. The table shows USD/CAD at 1.4113.
  • The Bank of Canada unveiled a new five-year policy framework that keeps the 2% inflation target.

5. The takeaway

If someone asks you about markets today: "A policy-driven AI selloff hit stocks this week. Strong US jobs data then raised the odds of a Fed hike, so next week's CPI report is the next big test."
