Stocks

AI sell-off deepens before Nvidia reports

AI stocks dragged the US market lower on Monday, led by Nvidia. The S&P 500 (a basket of 500 large US companies, used as a market scoreboard) had a third straight loss. Nvidia fell 4.1% on Monday. Tonight is the first time interim CEO (temporary boss) Colette Kress will speak to Wall Street since taking over from Huang. Nvidia is setting up a new board committee (a small group of directors who check specific issues) for national security and global trade compliance. This comes after revelations about Huang's personal conduct. The stock pared early losses after Huang said he would be willing to return as CEO if the board decided that was best for shareholders (the company's owners). Earnings and revenue are expected to be up about 90% from a year ago. A beat and raise (results better than expected, and a higher outlook) is widely expected. The bigger question is earnings visibility beyond 2028, meaning how clearly the company can see its profits further into the future. Chip designers and AI infrastructure companies lose the most if guidance disappoints. Broadcom and CoreWeave held up better this morning. Watch the CEO-succession answer on tonight's call and any comment on China export rules.

Rates

Treasury yields stay above 5% as markets price Fed hikes

Rates are driving the market wobble as much as AI is. Hot inflation data (prices rising quickly) and comments from a Fed official pushed traders to bet on more aggressive hikes. The Fed is the US central bank, and a rate hike means it raises the interest rate to slow prices. October producer prices rose 0.3% on the month, a bit below forecasts. But the annual rate of 6.5% was the highest since November 2022. A 10-year Treasury auction (a sale of 10-year US government bonds) also drew soft demand, meaning fewer buyers than hoped. One prediction market put the odds of a 25-basis-point December hike at 89%. A basis point is one-hundredth of a percentage point, so 25 basis points is a quarter of a percentage point. The 2-year yield, which tracks Fed expectations most closely, was around 4.85% this morning. Higher yields hit long-duration growth stocks hardest, because their big profits are far away. They also keep fixed mortgage rates high. Watch remarks from Fed officials later today. Chair Warsh's comments on the 17th are a focus.

Canada

Canadian yields fall as rate-hike bets ease

The Bank of Canada raised its policy rate in October. Investors are watching whether prices that keep rising could force more rate hikes. Before the latest CPI (consumer price index, a main measure of inflation), money markets priced about a 60% chance of a December hike. The TSX (the main Canadian stock index) fell 1.1% on Monday to 35,722.13. On the TSX Venture Exchange (a market for smaller companies), materials fell 3.6% and energy lost 2.4%. Gold stocks fell 2.8%. The TSX has many banks, energy companies and mining companies, so it moves a lot when commodity prices (oil, metals and similar goods) swing and when interest rate expectations change. Watch whether softer Canadian price pressures hold up in later data. A wider gap between US and Canadian rates also tends to keep the loonie (Canada's dollar) under pressure.

Acquisition (one company buys another) · About C$2.53 billion including debt (money the company owes)

Canadian Natural Resources → Advantage Energy

Canadian Natural will pay C$13.55 per share. That gives an equity value (the value of the shares) of about C$2.255 billion. The price is 25% above Advantage's Friday close, meaning what its shares last traded for at the end of the week. The deal will be done through a plan of arrangement (a legal process used to restructure a company). It is expected to close in Q1 2027, the first quarter of 2027. It will be funded from cash on hand and existing bank credit facilities (pre-approved loans from banks). The deal brings about 79,000 boe/d of production. Boe/d means barrels of oil equivalent per day, a way to measure oil and gas output. Management points to scale synergies (savings from being bigger) and better area efficiency. Canadian Natural says it will keep its free cash flow allocation policy, meaning how it plans to use the cash the business generates. Canadian Natural's shares fell about 2.5% in Toronto. It is one of several recent Canadian oil and gas tie-ups.

Acquisition (reported, not confirmed) · About $5 billion, according to the FT (Financial Times)

Diamondback → Double Eagle drilling portfolio (reported, not confirmed)

Only the FT's reported size and parties are available so far. No terms have been confirmed. Deals like this and Canadian Natural's purchase create public price benchmarks (reference prices) for producing wells and undeveloped drilling locations. The pattern across both deals is producers consolidating their best basins (big oil and gas regions). This signals confidence in oil-linked cash flows, even as oil trades near $90 a barrel.

Your interview line

Falling AI stocks and Treasury yields above 5% are pushing on each other. Nvidia's results and the odds of a December Fed rate hike are the two things that could move markets most.
Nvidia's leadership change shows how much a company's strategy can depend on one founder. Firms like it should have succession plans (plans for who leads next) and compliance oversight (checks that rules are followed) in place before a crisis forces them.
Canadian Natural's purchase of Advantage would count as a business combination. The buyer records the assets and liabilities at fair value (their current market value). The extra price paid above book value (the value on the company's books) would go to goodwill (an intangible asset) or to oil and gas properties. Auditors would focus on the reserve-based valuations (estimates of how much oil and gas is recoverable).

Words to know

Yield

The interest rate a bond pays, shown as a yearly percentage.

Earnings (earnings report)

A company's profit, and the report where it shares how much it made in a set period.

Guidance

A company's own forecast of how it expects to do in the future.

Risk-off

A mood where investors sell riskier assets like stocks and buy safer ones like bonds or cash.

Basis point

One-hundredth of a percentage point, so 25 basis points is a quarter of a percentage point.

Acquisition

When one company buys another company, or part of one.
