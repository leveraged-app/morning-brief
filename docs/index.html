1. The numbers

Risk-off, driven by tech. One caution on the table: the S&P 500 and VIX figures match Thursday's AI rout (Nov 5). On Friday, the S&P 500 rose 0.31% to 7,376.86. The Nasdaq figure is Friday's rebound. The WTI level also looks off, because reports put the Friday settle near $90.83. Treat the table as mixed-date data.

2. What moved and why

AI and chip stocks crashed Thursday, then partly bounced Friday

Why it happened: Democratic midterm wins fed fears of tighter US regulation of the AI industry. The Philadelphia semiconductor index fell more than 12%. The VIX jumped 69.7% to 28.18.

Why it matters: A few giant tech names drive index returns, so a policy scare in one theme can hit the whole market. The S&P 500 still had its worst week since June.

A much stronger US jobs report raised Fed hike odds

Why it happened: The US added 165,000 jobs in October against about 59,000 expected, and unemployment fell to 4.1%. Yields rose, strengthening the case for another Fed hike next month.

Why it matters: Good news on jobs can hurt stocks if it keeps rates higher. That is the rate-expectations link interviewers often ask about.

Oil had its biggest weekly drop since 2022

Why it happened: The election raised hopes for an end to the Iran war. Oil prices drive inflation expectations, and lower oil helped pull yields down earlier in the week.

Why it matters: Oil feeds inflation, central bank policy and Canada's currency and energy sector. Check the exact WTI level against the table note above.

3. What's coming today

  • Today is Saturday, so US and Canadian markets are closed, and no major data, central bank events or earnings are scheduled.
  • Next week's key event is US October CPI on Tuesday, Nov 10 at 8:30 a.m. ET. Forecasts call for headline inflation to ease to 0.3% and core to 0.2%.
  • Monday: the Bank of Canada's Q3 Market Participants Survey at 10:30 a.m. ET. Barrick and Constellation Energy report earnings.
  • Later in the week: Cisco, Applied Materials and CoreWeave report. On Nov 11, Canadian and US stock markets are open but bond markets are closed.

4. Canada corner

  • TSX: The Friday close isn't available. The last level I found was 36,249.03 on Oct 29.
  • Jobs: Canada lost 4,700 jobs in October, while economists had expected a gain of 13,600. Unemployment rose to 6.7%.
  • Bank of Canada: It raised rates in late October, its first hike in more than three years. One economist argued the weak labour trend supports a hold at the December meeting.
  • Loonie: USD/CAD rose to 1.4113, a weaker Canadian dollar. Falling oil and a stronger US economy are the likely drivers. That is my read, not something a source stated.
