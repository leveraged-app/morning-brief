Stocks

Nvidia drops after CEO Jensen Huang resigns

Nvidia has been the heart of this year's AI-driven rally. The news arrived a day after a rebound led by chip stocks. The Nasdaq (a stock index full of tech companies) jumped 1.60%, and the S&P 500 (a index of 500 large US companies) gained 1.31%. Nvidia fell 14% when trading opened. Then it recovered some ground and dropped about 7%. By late morning it was down about 5.6%. At least four brokers (firms that advise people on buying and selling stocks) cut their rating on the stock. They worry an investigation could last five months or longer, and there is no clear plan for who leads next. The big indexes shrank their early losses and turned positive. The reaction was mixed across chip stocks. Intel and Micron fell, while AMD rose. Broadcom also went up. Nvidia has many deals with other AI companies, so it is hard to know how far the damage will spread. The stock has dropped roughly 30% over the past month. Watch for a permanent CEO announcement and any news about the investigation. Tonight's results from Cisco and Applied Materials will also show how strong AI demand is.

Commodities

Gold slides as traders price a December Fed hike

Gold had been hovering around $4,000. It dropped back below that level for the third time since late October. Tuesday's hotter US inflation report started the move. A stronger dollar makes gold more expensive for buyers outside the US. Higher Treasury yields (the interest rate the US government pays on its bonds, which are loans to the government) make gold less appealing too. The drop spread across other metals. Silver fell 3.47% and copper slipped 1.8%. North American gold ETFs (funds that hold gold and trade like stocks) lost $512 million in October. That was the only region with money leaving, according to the World Gold Council. People who hold dollars and short-term bonds gain when yields rise. Gold mining companies, which make up a big part of Canada's stock market, face a weaker gold price. Next up is Friday's producer price report (a measure of inflation at the wholesale level). One strategist thinks gold could fall toward $3,870, near its late-October low, if the report comes in above forecasts.

Crypto

Bitcoin falls to its lowest since August

Bitcoin has moved with tech stocks for most of this month. Selling started after Wednesday's market close and picked up through the morning. Strategy is the largest company holder of bitcoin. Its shares fell about 7% at the open after MSCI confirmed it would be removed from its indexes. US spot bitcoin ETFs (funds that hold real bitcoin and trade like stocks) saw $299.8 million leave on Wednesday. That was their fifth straight day of money leaving, with about $1.44 billion gone over those five days. Leverage (borrowed money used to make bigger bets) made the drop worse. One count put crypto liquidations (forced sales of positions that lost too much value) at $656 million. The Binance Fear & Greed Index (a score of how scared or greedy crypto traders are) stood at 24, which signals extreme fear. Traders who borrow to bet lose the most in a drop like this. Funds that lost their index spot or face money leaving lose their steady buyers. One analyst points to support (a price level where buyers often step in) near the 200-day average (the average price over the last 200 trading days) and a zone between $70,000 and $71,500. Watch ETF money flows and Friday's PPI report.

Marvell → Lumentum

Lumentum holders get 4.5 Marvell shares for each share they own. They would own about 33% of the combined company. At announcement, that implied roughly $1,132 per Lumentum share. Marvell expects more than $1 billion a year in cost synergies (savings from combining the two companies) and says the deal will add to earnings (make earnings per share higher) in year one. Marvell fell almost 13% and Lumentum fell 18% on the news. The deal is more than ten times Marvell's previous largest deal. The expected savings only partly support the price. The deal shows how big companies are merging to build AI data center gear.

Words to know

Yield (the interest rate on bonds)

The return an investor earns from a bond, shown as a percentage. When yields rise, bonds look more attractive and gold often looks less attractive.

Fed rate hike

When the US central bank (the Fed) raises its main interest rate to slow down price increases, which makes borrowing more expensive.

ETF (exchange-traded fund)

A fund that holds a basket of assets, like gold or bitcoin, and trades on a stock exchange just like a single stock.

Leverage

Using borrowed money to make a bigger bet, which can grow gains but also makes losses larger.

Index

A group of stocks chosen to measure how a part of the market is doing, such as the S&P 500 for big US companies.

Downgrade (analyst rating cut)

When a broker lowers its opinion of a stock, often suggesting investors should be more cautious about it.
