Stocks

AI trade bounces back and lifts the Nasdaq

Last week's selloff included the Nasdaq's worst session in 18 months. Today's bounce is a partial reversal (only part of the drop was won back), with the Nasdaq up 1.90% and the S&P 500 (an index of 500 large US companies) up 1.26%. The catalyst was CoreWeave. The neocloud company (a newer type of cloud computing firm built for AI) beat third-quarter revenue expectations and raised its 2026 operating income and revenue guidance (its official forecast for the year). That helped Nvidia, which was at about $175 and up 4%. The Bloomberg wrap (a daily market summary) also cited the OpenAI revenue figure and the Anthropic IPO demand as more evidence. Winners are AI infrastructure names (companies that build the basic systems AI runs on). The Dow was down slightly at the open. The Russell 2000 (an index of smaller US companies) stayed under pressure, so the move was not even across the market. The VIX dropped to 17.77. Watch whether the rally spreads beyond the biggest tech companies. Also watch whether rate worries return, since higher yields (the interest rate on bonds) make fast-growing companies look less valuable.

Fed hike bets build after hot data

The Fed raised rates in September. Two officials voted for another hike in October. Since then, the data has not made a pause easy to argue for. October CPI (the main measure of consumer prices) was released yesterday. The index came in at 337.51, against a consensus (the average forecast of economists) of 337.02. That is a small beat on the headline index level. Economists had said a core print (inflation without food and energy) of 0.3% or higher would strengthen the case for a December hike. The US 10-year yield (the interest rate on the US 10-year government bond) sits at 4.88%. Stocks have so far looked past the talk of hikes. The bounce in tech shows investors are weighing AI earnings more heavily than rates today. Next up is a run of Fed speakers on Thursday, led by Williams. Hammack, who voted against the majority in October, speaks that morning. Producer prices (a measure of what companies pay for goods) come out on Friday.

FX

Dollar firms, loonie slips

Higher expected US rates tend to support the dollar against other currencies. That fits today's pattern, where the US dollar index and USD/CAD both moved up. The Canadian 10-year yield is 3.63%, well below the US 10-year at 4.88%. A wide gap like this favours the US dollar, because investors can earn more by holding US bonds. Oil is near $100 a barrel (WTI 99.64). WTI is a common type of oil. Canadian energy exports are priced in US dollars, so a weaker loonie lifts their value in Canadian dollars. Importers, and anyone with US-dollar debt (money borrowed in US dollars), lose out. Watch Thursday's Fed speakers and Friday's PPI. A hotter read would likely push the dollar higher again.

Acquisition (one company buys another) · About $115B

Marvell → Lumentum

Marvell's CEO called the deal a once-in-a-generation move to create a dominant AI connectivity player. Payment is entirely in stock, so Lumentum shareholders become owners of the combined company. The sources I found do not report a per-share price or premium (the extra amount paid above the current share price). The reason is vertical integration (owning more steps of the same supply chain). Data centres (buildings full of computers) need faster links between chips, and Marvell is paying to own more of that setup. Size matters here. At $115B, this is among the biggest deals in the sector. It shows that AI infrastructure is still attracting large investments from companies. It also shows that company bosses are willing to use their highly valued stock to pay for deals.

Acquisition (one company buys another) · Up to C$2.9B including debt

Strathcona Resources → Greenfire Resources

The deal values Greenfire at C$2.7 billion to C$2.9 billion, including debt (money the company owes). Greenfire shareholders can choose either 0.25 of a Strathcona share or $11 in cash per share. Greenfire closed at $7.93 on Friday, so the cash option is about a 39% premium (the extra amount paid above the current share price, my calculation). The stock option is worth about $9.09 at Strathcona's $36.34 close. Waterous Energy Fund holds 72% of Greenfire and 57% of Strathcona. It backs the deal and plans to take shares. Greenfire's leadership will keep running its assets as a Strathcona business unit (a part of the bigger company). It shows that Canadian oil sands companies (firms that get oil from thick sand deposits) keep joining together, and one owner sits on both sides.

Full story

Acquisition (one company buys another) · About C$226M

National Bank of Canada → Embark Student Corp.

The price is estimated at $226 million, subject to working capital adjustments (small changes based on the company's cash and short-term money at closing). The bank expects its CET1 ratio (a key measure of a bank's financial strength) to fall by about 10 basis points (0.10%) at closing. The bank will buy the shares from the Foundation. The reason is to add a digital RESP platform. Embark will keep applying for government grants for customers and investing RESP funds. The small effect on the bank's strength shows Canadian banks can still make smaller deals without putting strain on their finances.

Words to know

Yield

The interest rate a bond pays, shown as a percentage of its price.

Rate hike

When the central bank raises its key interest rate to slow the economy and fight rising prices.

VIX

A gauge of how nervous or calm investors are about the stock market; a higher number means more fear.

Acquisition

When one company buys all or part of another company.

Premium

The extra amount a buyer pays above a company's current share price to win a deal.

Oversubscribed

When more people want to buy shares in an offering than there are shares available.
